# NG Story Bible

## Working title

**No Gods in the Pines**

Alternative internal titles:

- **The Wolfpine Testament**
- **Saints of Mud and Iron**
- **The Road That Eats the Dead**
- **The Ash Beneath Wolfpine**

## Core promise

A small party of dangerous nobodies enters a starving borderland where every crime has a practical reason, every holy oath has a political cost, and every local grievance is tied to something older, buried, and hungry.

The story should feel mundane and epic at once:

- a missing flour wagon matters because a village will starve
- a butchered mule matters because its belly is full of finger bones and toll coins
- a corrupt captain matters because she is the only person still holding the road together
- a ranger matters because she knows which paths demand blood and which stories keep children alive
- a paladin matters because his oath is real, his order is compromised, and his mercy has limits
- a peasant murder matters because the dead do not always stay quiet in Wolfpine

The player should rarely be asked, "Are you good or evil?" The better campaign question is:

**What are you willing to make worse today to prevent something worse tomorrow?**

## Tone pillars

The game is dark, violent, and criminal, but not empty cruelty. Gore should usually be evidence, aftermath, warning, ritual, or consequence.

Emotional texture:

- mud, hunger, oath, debt, candlelight, old pine, wet iron, legal paper, grave ash
- practical people doing terrible things for understandable reasons
- good characters with limits, blind spots, and dirty hands
- criminals who sometimes keep people alive better than the law does
- holy institutions that preserve civilization while hiding founding crimes
- old powers that are not simple demons, but debts with memory

Small scenes matter as much as epic reveals:

- a ranger sewing a boot with fishing line beside a corpse fire
- a paladin washing blood from his chain shirt before prayer
- smugglers hiding fever medicine below salted eel barrels
- a child memorizing toll routes so she can rob travelers honestly
- a village reeve forging grain ledgers to save his daughter
- a dead bandit with pine needles growing from his tongue

## Expandable story structure

This story must be easy to work on constantly. New content should start local and practical, then connect to one or more deeper layers.

### Layer 1: ordinary pressure

Use grounded conflicts as entry points:

- missing caravans
- food theft
- road toll extortion
- unpaid soldiers
- poaching
- protection rackets
- bodies in rivers
- church courts
- forged ledgers
- disease rumors
- land seizure
- forced conscription
- deserters
- orphan gangs
- shrine relic fraud
- murdered tax collectors
- smugglers moving medicine
- smugglers moving corpses

### Layer 2: faction pressure

Every local problem should implicate at least two factions. Nobody controls the whole truth. Each faction needs:

- a public justification
- a private sin
- a service the region actually needs
- internal disagreement
- a reason decent people join
- a reason decent people leave
- routes to cooperate, expose, reform, exploit, or destroy it

### Layer 3: old law underneath

Wolfpine was not merely settled. It was sealed.

The old empire drove roads through a haunted pine sea and built its frontier on conquered shrines. Imperial saints did not simply defeat the powers below Wolfpine. They bargained, butchered, dismembered, renamed, and bound them into roads, bridges, granaries, shrines, relic courts, and mile-stones.

The empire called this civilization. The border folk inherited the locks without being told what they held shut.

## Campaign premise

The Wolfpine border road has gone bad.

Caravans vanish. Toll posts burn. Mules are found split open beside old imperial mile-stones, their bellies stuffed with pinecones, finger bones, and stamped copper toll discs. Villages blame bandits. Bandits blame unpaid soldiers. Soldiers blame smugglers. Smugglers blame the church. The church blames old sins.

Nobody is entirely wrong.

The player arrives at the edge of Wolfpine with little standing and a practical need to survive. The first job is simple: find what happened to a missing caravan before Wolfpine Village runs out of flour, salt, lamp oil, and patience.

The deeper truth is that the **Road Peace**, an ancient compact that keeps Wolfpine passable, is failing.

## Central mystery

### Surface question

Who is taking the caravans on Wolfpine Road?

### First answer

Several groups are taking them for different reasons:

- bandits steal food and horses
- soldiers hide losses and sell cargo
- smugglers divert medicine and relics
- village officials falsify ledgers
- the church quietly recovers forbidden bones
- something under the road accepts blood as toll

### Deeper answer

The missing caravans are symptoms of a failing covenant called the **Road Peace**: an old compact between imperial saints, border clans, ranger wardens, and the buried powers of Wolfpine.

The compact is maintained by mile-stones, shrines, toll ledgers, seasonal rites, ranger patrol patterns, church legal formulas, saint relics, and corpse law. Most people now perform pieces of these rites without understanding them.

### Final answer

The empire's holy history is false. The saints did not banish the old gods. They dismembered some, bargained with others, and used the remains to hold the frontier together.

The borderland is not cursed because old gods are returning. It is cursed because the institutions built on their corpses are still feeding.

## The Road Peace

The Road Peace is the mythic engine of the campaign. It is not purely magical and not purely political. It works because people keep acting as if the world has rules.

Components:

- **Mile-stones:** imperial stones carved with saint law, toll marks, and hidden binding geometry.
- **Shrines:** local maintenance points disguised as holy sites.
- **Ranger paths:** patrol routes that double as ritual circuits.
- **Toll ledgers:** tax records that preserve names, debts, deaths, and passage rights.
- **Saint relics:** bones, teeth, ash, weapons, and legal tokens of authority.
- **Corpse law:** burial, hanging, burning, naming, and testimony rules for the dead.
- **Road blood:** not metaphorical. The road has been paid in deaths for centuries.

Early failure signs:

- carts get lost on straight roads
- horses refuse bridges
- toll bells ring with no wind
- bodies are found too fresh after weeks missing
- letters arrive before they are sent
- dead bandits repeat their last lie
- pine roots grow through sealed stone
- a corpse leaves exact change at a toll gate

## Setting: the Wolfpine March

The Wolfpine March is a cold, poor, pine-choked border region at the edge of a tired successor realm. It is not a marble-tower kingdom. It is muddy roads, timber palisades, smoke, debt, old stones, bad harvests, rope law, and stubborn people.

Important locations:

- **Wolfpine Road:** the first artery of the campaign.
- **Wolfpine Village:** a settlement trying to look orderly while starving.
- **Collapsed Crypt:** a sealed burial and infrastructure site beneath an old shrine.
- **Grey Ranges:** pine ridges patrolled by ranger families, deserters, and things with old names.
- **Toll Houses:** fortified tax stations, some official, some criminal, some no longer human.
- **Blackfen:** marshland used by smugglers, corpse-sellers, runaway serfs, and illegal ferrymen.
- **Gallowmere:** a court town of rope-makers, tanners, hangmen, notaries, and church judges.
- **The Sainted Causeway:** a broken raised road built over old sacrifices.
- **The Ash Cathedral:** a half-ruined religious fortress around the oldest sealed crime in the March.

Daily life should be concrete. People are waiting for flour, dry socks, axle pins, lamp oil, medicine, fair judgments, and news from sons who joined patrols and never came back.

## Campaign acts

### Act I — Mud and Toll

The party arrives on Wolfpine Road and investigates the missing caravan.

Core beats:

- meet Captain Renna, Brannoc, border bandits, road refugees, shrine witnesses, and Wolfpine villagers
- discover that the caravan carried supplies, a hidden relic, and at least one person whose name was erased from the ledger
- choose what happens to the food, the surviving culprits, the first relic, and the public story
- open the first collapsed crypt or under-road chamber

Purpose: prove the game identity through grounded crime, ugly choices, companion reactions, faction pressure, and mythic unease.

### Act II — Hunger Courts

The party travels between settlements to reopen the road while famine politics intensify.

Core beats:

- resolve disputes between villagers, rangers, smugglers, church courts, soldiers, nobles, and criminals
- see how hunger changes morality
- recruit more companions
- discover contradictions in saint history
- learn that multiple factions want control of the failing Road Peace

Purpose: dense CRPG middle with side quests, companion arcs, reputation routing, faction choices, and multiple solutions.

### Act III — Saints Below

The party learns that the Road Peace is not merely failing. It is being deliberately broken by people who disagree about what should replace it.

Core beats:

- explore saint tombs, plague villages, under-road engines, ruined forts, and faction strongholds
- face paladin schisms, ranger betrayals, noble purges, criminal opportunism, and corpse testimony
- decide whether ancient bindings should be repaired, rewritten, exploited, confessed, or destroyed
- force companions to define what their codes actually mean under pressure

Purpose: the player understands enough to make large, informed mistakes.

### Act IV — No Gods in the Pines

The final conflict centers on the Ash Cathedral and the oldest sealed crime beneath the road.

Core beats:

- factions converge on the failing heart of the Road Peace
- prior choices determine who arrives, who betrays, who sacrifices, and who must be killed
- the player chooses the future relationship between law, hunger, the dead, old powers, and ordinary people

Possible end states:

- restore the Road Peace by preserving the lie and feeding it new law
- confess the saint-crime and build a harsher but more honest order
- break the compact and let villages survive without imperial roads
- bind the old powers into a new political settlement
- weaponize the Road Peace and become the thing every faction fears
- fail, leaving safe islands in a March where roads eat names

## Major factions

### Roadwardens

Official patrols responsible for roads, tolls, escorts, and bandit suppression.

Public face: tired professionals keeping commerce alive.

Private sin: falsified death ledgers, sold confiscated cargo, and quiet offerings to things under the road.

Internal tendencies:

- Captain Renna: practical order, ugly bargains, protect locals first
- Marshal Vargan: militarized control, hangings, collective punishment
- ledger-men: accountants who know records have ritual force

Player routes: stabilize them, expose them, replace leadership, militarize the road, or break them in favor of local militias.

### Grey Rangers

Border guides, hunters, scouts, monster-cullers, and old-route keepers. They are not romantic guardians of nature. They are survival experts who know the forest can be beautiful and merciless.

Public face: grim protectors who know the old paths.

Private sin: selective killing, abandoned innocents, and silence around old clan bargains.

Internal tendencies:

- Pathkeepers: preserve ancient route law no matter the cost
- Green Knives: cut nobles and church law out of the forest
- Hearth Rangers: protect villages even if old rites fail

Player routes: preserve their authority, reveal their crimes, reform them, back radicals, or bind them into a new settlement.

### The Sainted Lance

A paladin order sworn to defend roads, pilgrims, witnesses, and the lawful dead.

Public face: holy warriors and legal protectors.

Private sin: their founding miracle was also a massacre and a cover-up.

Internal tendencies:

- White Oath: law must hold, even when mercy suffers
- Ash Oath: truth matters more than institutional survival
- Red Oath: evil must be destroyed before courts can argue over it

Player routes: restore the order, expose and shatter it, weaponize it, or turn individual paladins toward mercy, law, vengeance, or apostasy.

### The Ash Church

The dominant religious institution in the March. It keeps burial law, marriage records, shrine rites, famine alms, orphan houses, and legal testimony.

Public face: social order, rites, education, and charity.

Private sin: edited history, hidden relic crimes, and corpses treated as legal objects.

Internal tendencies:

- parish priests: local, exhausted, often sincere
- reliquary courts: bureaucratic, secretive, legally ruthless
- penitent radicals: confess the church's crimes or burn it down

Player routes: use church law, expose the reliquary courts, steal relics, back reformers, or empower fanatics.

### Blackfen Free Company

Smugglers, river thieves, illegal ferrymen, corpse-haulers, counterfeiters, and medicine runners.

Public face: criminals.

Private truth: they move supplies the official road cannot or will not move.

Private sin: body trafficking, shortage manipulation, and paid betrayals.

Internal tendencies:

- medicine runners: criminals with limits
- corpse factors: no limits, only margins
- river knives: protection racketeers with boats

Player routes: use them, regulate them, destroy their leadership, legalize their logistics, or let them become a shadow government.

### Baronial Houses

Local nobility descended from imperial officers, clan traitors, and successful bandits.

Public face: law, land, defense, tax authority.

Private sin: inherited stolen land and secret knowledge of the Road Peace.

Internal tendencies: starving lesser houses, predatory great houses, embarrassed reformers, debt-bound heirs.

Player routes: broker settlements, back a claimant, incite revolt, force concessions, or expose original land thefts.

### The Rat Crown

A network of orphan gangs, gutter messengers, pickpockets, shrine-sleepers, and child informants.

Public face: nuisance crime.

Private truth: they know everything moving through villages and roads.

Private sin: older children sell younger ones into service, danger, or worse when winter comes.

Player routes: protect them, use them as spies, punish their leaders, place them under another faction, or recruit a former member.

### The Borrowed

A death cult, memory conspiracy, and survivor network depending on who describes them. They believe the dead are resources, witnesses, debts, and citizens wrongly excluded from law.

Public face: necromancers and grave criminals.

Private truth: they can recover testimony from the dead and expose crimes no living court will touch.

Private sin: consent becomes flexible when history feels urgent.

Player routes: destroy them, use them, legalize corpse testimony, or let them rewrite history with the mouths of the dead.

## Principal antagonists

### Marshal Edran Vargan

Hardline Roadwarden commander.

Belief: fear is cheaper than famine.

He wants road order restored through hangings, seized grain, forced labor, and military law. If empowered, roads may become safer and villages more terrified.

### Bishop-Curator Sanneth Vale

Senior Ash Church official responsible for relic law.

Belief: the truth can destroy more souls than a lie.

She knows saint history is edited. She will kill, blackmail, and erase people to prevent panic and preserve the Road Peace.

### Mother Grell of Blackfen

Smuggler matriarch, river boss, and illegal medicine supplier.

Belief: law is what rich people call a locked pantry.

She saves lives by breaking law and destroys lives by monetizing desperation.

### Sir Caulder Vey

Paladin of the Red Oath.

Belief: mercy before victory is vanity.

He is brave, incorruptible in money, and increasingly monstrous in method.

### The Witness Under Stone

An ancient bound power, god-remnant, saint-victim, or legal entity under the road.

Belief: debts must be paid by name.

It remembers every toll, body, oath, and betrayal used to build the Road Peace. It does not want simple destruction. It wants accounts settled.

## Companion design

Companions should react to public murder, torture, stealing food, betraying employers, helping children, desecrating corpses, lying in court, working with smugglers, empowering church law, destroying useful institutions, and mercy toward dangerous enemies.

Avoid a single approval meter when story flags would be clearer. Useful flags:

- `companion_saw_player_execute_prisoner`
- `companion_helped_bury_bandits`
- `companion_personal_truth_revealed`
- `companion_oath_shift_mercy`
- `companion_oath_shift_vengeance`
- `companion_left_due_to_corpse_law`

### Brannoc — caravan hand

Role: starter companion, drover, bruiser, practical survivor.

Public reputation: unlucky caravan man who keeps surviving other people's disasters.

Private wound: he once abandoned trapped passengers during a raid and has stayed useful ever since so nobody asks what kind of man he is.

Moral pressure: he hates waste more than violence. He can forgive theft if it feeds people. He struggles to forgive needless cruelty.

Possible endings: protector of travelers, criminal quartermaster, confessed coward seeking judgment, or broken denier exposed by evidence.

### Sella Wren — realistic ranger

Role: ranger, tracker, archer, pathfinder, trap specialist.

Public reputation: wolf-killer, poacher-hanger, forest guide.

Private wound: she helped enforce an old ranger rite that sacrificed a guilty man and an innocent child to keep a winter path open. She tells herself both would have died anyway.

Why she is good: she protects isolated farms and remembers every missing child's name.

Why she is dangerous: she believes survival knowledge gives her authority over softer people.

Possible endings: reformer of the Grey Rangers, full Pathkeeper, exposed exile, or bridge between rangers and courts.

### Brother Maeric — tired paladin

Role: paladin, shield fighter, field medic, witness-protector.

Public reputation: gentle for a holy knight, which some call weakness.

Private wound: he once spared a raider who later burned a roadside hospice. Since then, mercy feels like a loaded weapon.

Why he is good: he believes law exists to protect the weak from private vengeance.

Why he is dangerous: when he decides someone is beyond law, he becomes terrifyingly calm.

Possible endings: renewed mercy oath, hardline execution doctrine, oathless protector, or broken deserter from the party.

### Ilyra Voss — gutter advocate

Role: rogue, court-scribe, lockpicker, blackmailer, legal trickster.

Public reputation: liar, document thief, former Rat Crown girl.

Private wound: she sold a younger child's hiding place to survive winter and became obsessed with legal names because nameless children vanish too easily.

Why she is good: she weaponizes paperwork for people who cannot swing swords.

Why she is dangerous: she treats truth as raw material.

Possible endings: legal shelter founder, ruthless spymaster, penitent seeking restitution, or betrayer of institutions that erase the poor.

### Durn Halvik — deserter with useful hands

Role: fighter, sapper, axeman, camp repair, siege craft.

Public reputation: deserter, murderer, drunk.

Private wound: he killed his officer during a forced grain seizure, then killed a witness to escape.

Why he is good: he knows what violence costs and tries to stop boys from becoming soldiers.

Why he is dangerous: he is very good at solving problems permanently.

Possible endings: trial and consequence, militia founder, player executioner, or deserter from an atrocious party.

### Nessa Tallow — ratcatcher apothecary

Role: healer, poisoner, plague spotter, vermin expert.

Public reputation: filthy miracle-worker.

Private wound: she tested a plague remedy on condemned prisoners and still keeps the notes because the remedy worked.

Why she is good: she saves people nobody else touches.

Why she is dangerous: she can rationalize almost anything as research if bodies are already doomed.

Possible endings: principled field physician, licensed monster under church protection, destroyer of her notes, or publisher of a medical breakthrough with a horrific origin.

### Ser Joric Anvil-Saint — paladin who believes too much

Role: offensive paladin, anti-undead, morale anchor.

Public reputation: brave, loud, beloved by peasants, mocked by elites.

Private wound: he knows some miracles are lies but fears people will become worse without them.

Why he is good: he gives courage freely and stands between mobs and scapegoats.

Why he is dangerous: he may defend a beautiful lie after it starts killing people.

Possible endings: honest folk-saint, institutional martyr, fanatic defender of the lie, or broken man who learns humility.

## First playable module story

### Missing Caravan

Setup: Captain Renna needs the party to investigate a missing supply caravan before Wolfpine Village starves or riots.

Key discoveries:

1. Bandits hit the caravan but did not take everything.
2. One wagon was diverted before the ambush.
3. The cargo manifest has one erased passenger name.
4. The old shrine shows fresh ruts, blood, and a toll disc pressed into a dead mule's eye.
5. A survivor is hiding, guilty of something but not the main crime.
6. The caravan carried flour, salt, lamp oil, and a sealed reliquary box.

Possible resolutions:

- return supplies to Wolfpine Village and strengthen Renna
- sell or trade supplies through Blackfen contacts
- expose Renna's falsified ledgers but risk village panic
- give the relic to the church, rangers, smugglers, or keep it
- execute, spare, recruit, or publicly try the surviving culprit

Consequences:

- food prices shift
- Renna's trust changes
- bandits may retaliate or negotiate
- church attention increases
- Brannoc reacts strongly to waste, cruelty, and abandoned travelers
- the first Road Peace failure flag is set

### Old Shrine

The shrine should begin as a hotspot and grow into a durable story node.

Scene elements:

- cracked saint-stone
- pine roots through carved law text
- old wax below fresh blood
- wagon ruts that stop too cleanly
- a hidden ledger niche
- a corpse that has paid a toll after death

Expandable uses:

- tutorial investigation site
- first corpse-law choice
- first ranger clue
- first church-cover-up clue
- later haunting if neglected
- optional combat if the player breaks the wrong seal

### Wolfpine Village

Wolfpine Village should not be a generic starter town. It is a pressure cooker.

Core conflicts:

- food shortage
- road closure
- Renna's emergency authority
- priest versus reeve over public truth
- smugglers offering practical help at a criminal price
- frightened villagers looking for scapegoats
- children tracking rumors faster than adults
- a corpse disposal dispute after the caravan evidence returns

## Quest-writing template

Every significant quest should answer:

1. What is the ordinary problem?
2. Who benefits if nothing changes?
3. Which factions are implicated?
4. What is the first lie?
5. What is the sympathetic reason behind the lie?
6. What is the old-law or mythic layer?
7. What can be solved by violence?
8. What cannot be solved by violence?
9. Which companion cares most and why?
10. What changes in the world state afterward?

## Writing rules

- Start quests with material stakes before supernatural stakes.
- Do not make good characters naive. Make them costly.
- Do not make evil characters stupid. Make them useful.
- Let crime be functional, not decorative.
- Let paladins and rangers be frightening when their virtues harden.
- Let mercy sometimes fail and cruelty sometimes work, but remember who had to live with it.
- Use gore as evidence, ritual, consequence, or horror punctuation, not constant wallpaper.
- Prefer names, debts, ledgers, roads, graves, tools, weather, food, and bodies over abstract lore dumps.
- Let the player become important through accumulated consequences, not chosen-one destiny.
